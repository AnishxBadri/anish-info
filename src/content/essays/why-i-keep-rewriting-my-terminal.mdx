I have rewritten the same small terminal tool four times. Not because the first three were broken, but because every version showed me something about how I actually think while I work. The tool is a mirror I keep polishing. This essay is about what each rewrite taught me, and why I think building your own instruments is an underrated way to learn a craft.

## Why a terminal

The terminal is the most honest interface I use. There is no layout to hide behind. You type, it answers. When I started learning Rust<Sidenote>Mostly through the Rust Book, and a lot of fighting with the borrow checker at 1am.</Sidenote> I wanted a project small enough to finish and strange enough to keep me curious, so I built a tiny TUI that shows my notes, my reading list and my to-dos in one pane.<Sidenote>Built with ratatui. The first version was 212 lines and panicked on resize.</Sidenote> It was ugly, and it was mine.

<PullQuote>Every rewrite was less about the code and more about noticing what I actually reached for.</PullQuote>

## Borrowing, literally

<BookRef id="working-in-public" />

Around the third rewrite I was reading Nadia Eghbal's *Working in Public*, and one idea stuck: most software is maintained by very few people who care a great deal. That changed how I thought about my little tool. I was not building a product. I was maintaining a relationship with my own habits.
